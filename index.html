About Me

Structural biology · Photosynthesis · Cryo-electron microscopy

Hello! I'm Kira DeVore, a PhD candidate in Biochemistry at Arizona State University, working under the mentorship of Dr. Po-Lin Chiu.

My research focuses on structural biology and the application of cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM) to investigate the molecular mechanisms of proteins involved in photosynthesis. I am particularly interested in understanding protein structure and function, as well as developing computational methods to improve cryo-EM data analysis.

My current projects include investigating the structural interactions between Rubisco and Rubisco activase in higher plants, characterizing photosynthetic protein complexes from Chlorobaculum tepidum, and developing computational approaches to address challenges associated with tilted cryo-EM imaging.

I earned bachelor's degrees in Biochemistry (Medicinal Chemistry) and Biological Sciences (Genetics, Cell, and Developmental Biology) from Arizona State University in 2022.

Outside the laboratory, I enjoy rock climbing, running, and playing guitar.

Research

My research explores how photosynthetic proteins work and how we can improve the techniques used to visualize them.

Rubisco–Rubisco Activase Structural Biology

Plants use an enzyme called Rubisco to capture carbon dioxide from the atmosphere during photosynthesis. Another protein, Rubisco activase, helps keep this enzyme working efficiently. I use biochemical experiments and cryo-electron microscopy to study how these proteins interact and how their structures support carbon fixation.

Photosynthetic Protein Complexes

I study the photosynthetic bacterium Chlorobaculum tepidum to better understand how organisms capture and use light energy. My work includes investigating ATP synthase, the molecular machinery that produces a major source of energy for cells, and characterizing how photosynthetic systems respond to their environment.

Cryo-EM Method Development

Cryo-electron microscopy allows researchers to reconstruct detailed three-dimensional images of proteins from many two-dimensional views. Tilting specimens can reveal missing views, but it also introduces imaging distortions. I develop computational approaches to measure these effects and evaluate how they influence the accuracy of structural reconstructions.
